## Context

Sales calls for course enrolments had to meet consumer-protection rules that differ by country and by payment method. Compliance was checked by three near-identical low-code workflows, one per region, each over 60 steps long. Changing a rule meant editing three workflows and hoping they stayed in step. Analysis showed the regional prompts were 96% identical.

## What I built

A single pipeline inside the organisation's internal portal. A scheduled job picks up new enrolments, matches each one to its call transcript, and runs two stages:

1. **Extraction:** a fast, inexpensive model records what was said, with quotes.
2. **Assessment:** a stronger model decides, rule by rule, whether anything was breached.

Rules live in a database table, scoped by region and payment method, so compliance staff can change wording without a deployment. Breaches raise notifications, and every verdict is stored with its evidence.

## Key decisions

- **Two stages instead of one large prompt.** Finding statements and judging them are different jobs. Splitting them made errors traceable and let the cheap model do the high-volume work.
- **Rules and models as data.** A model-per-stage table means a model can be swapped without code changes, and every rule is versioned.
- **Learn from reviewers, not from past verdicts.** Reviewer confirm, dismiss and missed-breach feedback drives rule rewording. The alternative was storing past verdicts in a vector store, and I argued against it because it would quietly reinforce old mistakes.
- **Cost and failure telemetry per stage.** Token use and failures are tracked per tool, so cost surprises show up the same day.

## How quality was measured

A golden set of reviewed calls, scored on each change, with re-review tooling for disputed verdicts. Reviewer agreement and per-rule fire rates are tracked over time.

## Results

- Three regional workflows replaced by one config-driven pipeline.
- 43 rules managed as data rather than inside prompts.
- Thousands of calls a month: in one 30-day period, roughly 3,600 LLM calls and around 65 million input tokens across the two stages, and nearly 2,000 compliance reports produced in total.

## What I would do next

Calibrate confidence per rule against reviewer decisions, so only the uncertain band goes to people, and replay recorded calls nightly as a regression gate.

The public repository rebuilds this pattern on synthetic data, with its evaluation harness, review queue and CI gate.
