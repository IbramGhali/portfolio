## Context

Sales, HR, operations, student services, support and engineering each had their own tools, spreadsheets and low-code automations. AI was being added piecemeal, each feature with its own keys, prompts and logging.

## What I built

A monorepo of department apps on one shared database with row-level security, behind a single sign-in. It holds three AI capabilities on shared plumbing:

1. **A chat assistant platform.** Several assistants share a typed tool registry covering CRM, contact centre, compliance, grading, stock, recruitment and knowledge. They stream responses, redact personal information, rate-limit, and log usage per tool. Tools are read-only by default.
2. **A knowledge base.** A shared document folder is synced by checksum. Changed files are re-embedded, removed files are deleted, and a guard stops accidental mass removal.
3. **Scheduled AI pipelines.** Compliance review of sales calls, multimodal assignment grading, and support-ticket triage, all built on a job queue with failures routed to people.

## Key decisions

- **One tool registry, many assistants.** A new capability is one typed tool, available to any assistant that is allowed it, instead of a new bot.
- **Read-only first.** Assistants answer and draft; actions that change records stay with people.
- **Honesty over confidence.** The triage assistant's rule is to say when it is unsure, rather than guess a category.
- **Models as configuration.** A model-per-stage table and per-tool usage ledger made model changes and cost questions routine.

## How quality was measured

Golden sets for the compliance and grading pipelines, cost and failure dashboards per tool, and documentation generated from the source, so it could not drift.
