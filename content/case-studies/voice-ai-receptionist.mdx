## Context

Admissions staff spent much of the day answering the same questions and booking calls back. Calls outside office hours were lost.

## What I built

A voice assistant on a voice-AI platform with four tools exposed as webhooks:

1. contact lookup
2. availability
3. booking
4. callback scheduling

All four ran against the CRM's calendar. After each call, a processing workflow extracted structured fields from the transcript for reporting. When staff edited the knowledge document, the change was pushed to the assistant automatically.

## Key decisions

- **Tools, not prompt knowledge, for anything that changes.** Availability and bookings come from the calendar at call time, never from the prompt.
- **Knowledge as a document staff already edit.** Updating the assistant became editing a document, with no technical step.
- **Structured post-call data.** Every call produced the same fields, so reporting did not depend on reading transcripts.

## How quality was measured

Every call produced the same structured fields, so calls could be reviewed and reported on without reading transcripts. The project ran as a production-style pilot.

## Results

- Calls answered, availability checked and appointments booked by the voice agent, with every booking written to the CRM.
- Knowledge updates with no developer involvement.

## What I would do next

Move business rules (opening hours, double-booking prevention, who may see which record) out of the prompt and into a tested service. Make escalation categories mandatory and audited. That is the design of the public rebuild.

The public repository implements that version: a webhook service with a 30-scenario offline simulator, zero hallucinated bookings, and an n8n alternative.
